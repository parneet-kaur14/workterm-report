Introduction

This website provides a glimpse into my co-op work experience completed from May to December 2025 across two consecutive work terms (Summer 2025 – S25 and Fall 2025 – F25) at the Experiential Learning Hub (EL Hub), University of Guelph, where I worked as a Systems Support Technician.

What I hope readers will take away is how this extended experience evolved into a continuous learning journey that supported my growth both professionally and academically.

Throughout this period, I supported the Experience Guelph platform, which connects students, employers, and faculty through job postings and recruitment processes. My responsibilities included managing Experience Guelph support tickets, responding to student, employer, and faculty inquiries, and providing in-person IT support to EL Hub staff. I also escalated technical issues to Computing & Communications Services (CCS), the University’s central IT department, when required.

During S25, my focus was on learning core systems, understanding workflows, and building confidence through hands-on troubleshooting and documentation, including the creation of the student user guide for Experience Guelph. As I transitioned into F25, I took on increased responsibility by independently managing tickets and in-person support requests, leading a successful digital clean-up initiative, and updating employer and faculty advisor user guides. In addition, I documented repetitive tasks and recurring processes to support knowledge transfer and ensure that future co-op students could onboard more efficiently and work more independently.

This report shares my experiences, goals, and reflections across both work terms, highlighting how this extended role allowed me to grow from a learner into a confident and reliable contributor while building a strong foundation for future co-op experiences.

Employer

The Experiential Learning Hub (EL Hub) at the University of Guelph is the central department that connects students, employers, and faculty through career development and experiential learning opportunities. The EL Hub supports a wide range of initiatives, including Co-operative Education, Career Education, and the university's primary recruitment platform, Experience Guelph.

Through Experience Guelph, the EL Hub supports a broad user base by providing access to job postings, workshops, appointments, and career resources. Students can explore a variety of opportunities, including co-op positions, on-campus and off-campus jobs, volunteer roles, and opportunities available to graduate students and alumni.

Experience Guelph also offers features such as appointment booking for career and co-op advising, peer support, and registration for events and workshops. In addition, the platform provides access to user guides and resources that help users navigate experiential learning requirements and career planning more effectively.

A key component supported through Experience Guelph is the Professional and Career Development Record (PCDR), which allows students to track their skill development and reflect on experiences gained through co-op, work, and co-curricular activities.

During my co-op work experience, I supported the EL Hub by helping to maintain the Experience Guelph platform and assisting staff, students, and employers with technical and administrative inquiries. This role allowed me to contribute to the smooth operation of a system that supports experiential learning across the University of Guelph.

Job Description & Projects

Overview of My Role

As an Advancement Systems & Analysis Student Analyst, I worked on projects across the Technology & Development and Business Improvement & Training areas of ASA. My work combined software testing, automation, process improvement, SharePoint development, documentation, training, and stakeholder collaboration.

A major part of my role was learning unfamiliar technologies and applying them to real departmental needs. I worked with multiple project leads and directorates, regularly shared progress, gathered feedback, and presented or demonstrated solutions throughout the term.

1 Automated Testing with Playwright

My largest technical project involved developing automated end-to-end tests using Playwright and JavaScript for AA&D's online donation and tribute forms. The goal was to automate repetitive testing of important user workflows across different site configurations.

I contributed approximately half of the automated test cases, covering scenarios such as anonymous donations, mailed tribute letters, company donations, form validation, donation selections, and payment-related workflows. I worked with reusable helper functions, selectors, test data, debugging, and existing project code to make the tests reliable and maintainable.

This project gave me hands-on experience with real-world software testing and taught me how to design tests around complete user journeys rather than individual functions. I also presented and demonstrated the automation work, which helped me strengthen both my technical knowledge and my ability to explain technical solutions to others.

2 Document Management, Digital Cleanup & SharePoint

Another major area of my work was supporting the Document Management Framework and the department's digital cleanup initiative. I met with different directorates to understand how they currently stored and managed information, identify challenges, and gather requirements for possible improvements.

I helped prepare and deliver digital cleanup sessions, presentations, documentation, and supporting resources. I also created SharePoint demonstration sites for the department and individual directorates, exploring site structure, navigation, document libraries, metadata, resources, and ways to organize information more consistently.

This project gave me experience taking a problem from stakeholder discussions through research, design, demonstration, and feedback. It also showed me that successful technical solutions need to consider usability, governance, accessibility, and how people actually work with information.

3 PowerShell File Inventory & Reporting

I used PowerShell to support a large-scale file inventory project for a shared directory. The script collected information about the existing folder and file structure and generated reports that could be used to support future analysis and cleanup decisions.

The final inventory covered 12,397 folders and 547,221 files. Working at this scale introduced practical challenges such as execution time, very large output files, and making the resulting information usable for the stakeholder who requested it.

This project showed me how scripting can turn a task that would be impractical to complete manually into structured information that can support business decisions.

4 Power Automate & Workflow Improvement

I also worked on process-improvement initiatives using Power Automate and Microsoft Forms. This included examining an existing form-based process and exploring how information collection, conditional sections, and approval steps could be moved into a more streamlined Microsoft 365 workflow.

The project required me to first understand the existing business process before deciding what could realistically be automated. Exploring different approaches helped me learn that automation is not simply about replacing manual steps; the workflow also needs to account for requirements, approvals, maintainability, and the people who will use it.

5 Digital Awareness, Training & Presentations

Throughout the term, I created and delivered presentations, documentation, and training resources for staff with different levels of technical experience. This included researching digital best practices, organizing content, gathering feedback, and adapting materials so that technical information was clear and accessible.

I also explored the use of AI voiceover tools when creating video-based training resources. This gave me experience using emerging technology as part of content development while still focusing on the clarity, accessibility, and usefulness of the final resource.

Presenting and facilitating discussions throughout the term significantly improved my confidence communicating technical information and responding to questions from different audiences.
